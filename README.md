---

# Definition

Recursive Adaptive Dynamics (RAD) is a framework for analyzing systems where the mechanisms responsible for producing future change can themselves become objects of change.

RAD studies:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the evolution of reachable spaces of future adaptive mechanisms}
}
\]


---

# Scope Boundary

RAD does not claim to define:

- intelligence
- learning
- optimization
- alignment
- agency
- consciousness

RAD defines a causal framework for studying recursive systems in which mechanisms of change become objects of change.

---

# Core Distinction

\[
\boxed{
\text{change within a mechanism space}
\neq
\text{change of the mechanism space itself}
}
\]

A system can improve existing strategies without increasing its ability to generate future strategies.


---

# Ontology

## State

\[
X_t
\]

Current system state.


## Transformation mechanism

\[
F_t
\]

The process that transforms states.

\[
X_{t+1}=F_t(X_t)
\]


## Transformation-selection mechanism

\[
G_t
\]

The mechanism that modifies future transformations.

\[
F_{t+1}=G_t(F_t)
\]


## Adaptive mechanism space

\[
\mathcal{G}_t
\]

The reachable space of possible transformation-selection mechanisms.

\[
G_t\in\mathcal{G}_t
\]


## Internal model

\[
M_t
\]

Internal representations, memory, or models of consequences.


## Constraint information

\[
\Omega_t
\]

Information generated by consequences and environmental interaction.

# Hypothesis

RAD investigates:

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_t\rightarrow\Delta\mathcal{G}_{t+1}
}
\]

Meaning:

Long-horizon recursive adaptation may depend on whether viability-relevant constraints continue influencing the evolution of reachable future adaptive mechanisms.

This is a hypothesis, not an established fact.


---

# Research Question

When systems modify their own mechanisms of change, what determines whether future adaptive possibilities remain shaped by consequences?


---

# Simulator Objective

The simulator tests whether systems with:

\[
\Omega_t\rightarrow\Delta\mathcal{G}_{t+1}
\]

and systems without:

\[
\Omega_t\rightarrow\Delta\mathcal{G}_{t+1}
\]

develop different long-horizon adaptive trajectories.

---

# Experimental Principle

RAD does not test:

> Does feedback improve performance?

It tests:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does consequence-shaped evolution of adaptive mechanism spaces create measurable differences in future adaptation?}
}
\]

# Falsification

The central hypothesis is weakened if systems maintain long-horizon adaptive capacity under:

- sustained novelty
- changing constraints
- adversarial perturbations

while:

\[
\Omega\nrightarrow\Delta\mathcal{G}
\]
